Where enquiries are being lost

We went through your current site as a customer would. Each of these gives someone a reason to ring a competitor instead.

It looks unfinishedProduct pages show stock pictures stamped "SAMPLE", a blank placeholder image, and the homepage has a spelling slip ("Second Fix Timbe").
It disagrees with itselfThe homepage says 40+ years and the About page says over 30. Two different email addresses appear.
Your edge is buriedYou saw, dress and profile your own timber and can match a heritage moulding. The site barely says so.
Nothing asks for the jobThere are 356 products and no prices, so every sale depends on an enquiry. The site doesn't make that enquiry easy.

On a phone, where most people will find you

Now

New

  • Now: the header is cut off, the address wraps over three lines and a chat bubble covers the list.
  • New: one clear line, then two buttons: get a quote, or see timber in a room.
  • A bar with Ring and Get a quote stays within thumb reach on every page.
  • It shows whether the yard is open right now.

A customer who can't tell what you sell in five seconds leaves.

How it brings in more work

Every part of the new site has one job: move a visitor a step closer to ringing you or asking for a quote.

The 3D room

They see the floor before they buy it.

A customer picks any of your 17 timbers and sees it laid in a room: floor, door and skirting, in boards or herringbone. Nobody can picture a floor from a sample the size of a hand. This lets them, and none of your competitors' sites do it.

The timber rackAll 17 species side by side, each one click from the room or a quote.Pays by: keeping people comparing your timbers, not searching elsewhere.
The metres calculatorAn area and a board width become lineal metres, sent straight to the quote form.Pays by: turning "roughly this big" into a job you can price.
One board, start to floorAn animation of a board being sawn, dressed, profiled and laid.Pays by: proving you machine your own, which a big-box store can't say.
A quote form that reaches youShort, with the timber and metres already filled in. It only says "thanks" once delivered.Pays by: no enquiry lost between the website and your inbox.
Your work, up frontYour own floors, doors and decks run across the homepage and fill the gallery.Pays by: showing finished jobs, which is what sells a floor.
Found on Google, kept on GoogleOld page addresses point to the new ones, so existing search results keep working.Pays by: not losing the traffic you already have.

What we need from you

  • Which of the four looks you prefer
  • Years trading, the email for enquiries, and Saturday hours confirmed
  • A morning at the yard for photos of the boards, the machines and a few jobs
  • Your logo file and the three catalogue PDFs
  • Whether every product needs its own page, or one page per range is enough

Good to know

  • The demo uses your current photos. A shoot would lift it more than anything else.
  • The close-up timber images and the room are illustrations matched to your samples. Real photos replace them.
  • A better website can't promise sales. What it does is stop losing the enquiries your visitors were ready to make.
