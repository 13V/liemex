Logo directions · November 2026

Ten ways to redraw the Liemex logo

Drawn from what your own website says about the business. Each is one colour, so it can be cut in vinyl, embroidered, or branded onto a board. These are concepts to react to, not finished artwork.

Where it is now

A badge that's hard to use

  • The globe is a photograph, so it can't be cut, stitched or stamped, and blurs when small.
  • Three lines of text and an outlined name compete for attention.
  • The letters are see-through, so it changes with whatever is behind it.
  • The globe speaks to the importing side of the business. It doesn't say timber, or that you machine it here.

What your own site says

We went through your site before drawing anything

Who you areAn importer and maker, 30 years in. You saw, dress and profile on your own moulders, and your tradespeople build doors with mortise and tenon joints, plus fireplaces, vanities and benchtops.
What you're known forBaltic pine and jarrah come up again and again as "our specialty". Mouldings "have always been an important part" of the business: 79 profiles, every code starting with L.
Who buysThree groups. Resellers and trade, your stated market for 30 years. Homeowners renovating or building, especially period homes. Architects and commercial projects, who specify.
The problem to fix firstYou use three names: Liemex, Baltic Pine & European Timber, and Architectural Timber Products. The logo should lead with one. Each direction below shows Liemex leading, with the others as a line underneath.

The Profile, The Stamp and The Tenon.

The Profile and The Tenon come from things only you can claim: your L-coded mouldings and the way your doors are built. The Stamp is the gentlest change for customers who know the badge. Pick one, or the parts of each you like.
